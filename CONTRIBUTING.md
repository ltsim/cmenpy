# Contributing

Collaborate and contribute. For this software to progress, it must evolve, and that depends entirely on collective action. 

This project is a shared, borderless resource (*commons*) developed through decentralized cooperation. We operate under a strictly horizontal and democratic framework. Knowledge has no nationality, institutional boundaries, or academic hierarchies. Anyone—regardless of geographic location, academic degree, institutional affiliation, or whether they belong to LTSIM, CUCEI, or UDG—is welcome to participate and co-govern this codebase without restrictions.

---

## Ways to Contribute

All forms of contribution are fundamentally equal in value to the sustainability of this tool. You do not need to be an official member or a core software developer to make a meaningful impact:

1. **Research and Modeling:** Proposing theoretical improvements, mathematical optimization, or agent-based modeling frameworks.
2. **Data and Validation:** Sharing laboratory datasets, experimental trials, or helping with simulation calibration from any research group worldwide.
3. **Software Engineering:** Writing code, optimizing algorithms (AI, image processing, computing), fixing bugs, or writing unit tests.
4. **Documentation and Accessibility:** Improving documentation, writing tutorials, or translating content to bridge language barriers and make science accessible to everyone.

---

## Collective Workflow

To ensure cooperation remains smooth, autonomous, and free of centralized power structures, we follow these horizontal guidelines:

### 1. Open Dialogue
Before making major changes, please open an **Issue** in the repository to discuss your ideas with the collective. This open space allows for decentralized feedback and ensures we build upon mutual aid rather than duplication of effort.

### 2. General Standards
* Keep your contributions modular, clear, and well-documented so that anyone else can understand and build upon them.

### 3. Horizontal Review
* Fork the repository and submit a **Pull Request (PR)**. 
* **Keep your PR brief, concise, and direct.** Do not beat around the bush; clearly state what was changed, why it was changed, and what problem it solves in as few words as possible.
* Peer review is collaborative: any member of the community can participate in validating the contributions based on reproducibility and shared quality standards.

---

## Recognition

Once your contribution (code, data, modeling, or documentation) is integrated, please add your name to the **`CONTRIBUTORS.md`** file in strict alphabetical order. We ensure that credit and visibility are distributed equitably across the entire global collective.

* Thank you for joining us in dismantling elitism and advancing truly Open Science and Free Software across borders!
